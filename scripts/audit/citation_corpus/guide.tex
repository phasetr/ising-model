\section{Corpus}

A macro citation: \texttt{Corpus/Resolved.lean} resolves.
A path macro: \path{Corpus/Resolved.lean} resolves as well.
An escaped underscore: \texttt{Corpus/Under\_Score.lean} names a real file.
Brace shorthand: \texttt{Corpus/Pair/\{A, Gone\}.lean} is two citations.
A bare prose token: Corpus/Gone.lean points nowhere.
A bare basename: \texttt{Alone.lean} is evidence of a name, not of a path.
An ambiguous tail: \texttt{Twin.lean} matches two tracked files.
Glued: \texttt{../Corpus/Resolved.lean} and \texttt{Corpus/Resolved.lean.bak}.
A tilde in a component: \texttt{Corpus/Resolved~1.lean} names no file here.
A shorter tail: \texttt{Score.lean} is not a component of any tracked path.
Not citations: *.lean patterns and the .lean extension itself.

\begin{Verbatim}
Corpus/Wrapped/
Bridge.lean
+-- Corpus/
    Alone.lean
    Corpus/
Alone.lean
\end{Verbatim}

The result now lives in \texttt{Corpus/Moved.lean},
and it is re-exported for backward compatibility by
the old \texttt{Moved.lean} shim.
