## UPDATE 2026-07-26 (dev-pr-clerk, dev-perf ROI verdict) — #4724 prerequisite resolved; ROI = worth doing; implementation still gated

Per #4724's reconciliation (`.self-local/reports/perf-4724-fixed-cost-reconciliation.md`, comment
https://github.com/phasetr/ising-model/issues/4724#issuecomment-5082030683), the confirmed
per-module fixed-cost baseline is **`real` 2.22s / CPU 2.24s per module** (warm, serial, bare
`lean`); Measurement A's ~7.0s was inflated ~3.2x by `lake env` wrapper overhead + a partially-cold
OS page cache, both now identified and explained.

**Verdict: worth doing.** Remaining scope: **28 families ≈ 175 modules → 28 files ⇒ ~147 modules
eliminated**. At the confirmed value: CPU ≈ 147 × 2.24s ≈ **~330s**; clean-full-build wall ≈
147 × 0.63s (measured in-build effective per-module cost at 10-way parallelism) ≈ **~93s** (range
70–150s) ≈ **~9%** of the current 1022s clean full build. This is by far the largest remaining
build-time item — the entire 2026-07-24/25 hot-spot campaign (#4695 −7.6s, #4698 −2.4s, #4699
−0.9s, #4713 −11.47s, #4716 −22.09s, #4722 −3.34s) sums to an order of magnitude less than #4563's
~93s, and the conclusion survives Measurement A's 3.2x over-statement with a large margin. The
standard "incremental rebuild regresses" objection is empirically negligible here (2.22s → 2.53s
per family, because family content is ≈0.3s).

**The real cost is labour, not build time**: 28 separate multi-file PRs is a poor rate of return on
**review** effort at ~5.3s wall/family; recommend **batching 4–7 families per PR**.

**This comment records that the #4724 prerequisite is resolved and the investment judgement is
favourable — it is NOT an implementation authorization.** Per this issue's own body ("do NOT begin
work under generic `/goal` re-issue") and the still-open question of whether the 2026-07-18
standing blanket authorization remains in force, **item-specific user authorization is still
